Tinkercad to Bambu Studio

Station tutorial. Take your Tinkercad design and turn it into a real print on one of the Bambu printers. About 10 minutes if nothing goes wrong, which is a bold assumption.

Arrow keys also move between steps.

Something went wrong

What you seeWhat to do
Your printer isn't in the list when you press PrintThis laptop must be logged in to the Bambu account that owns the printers, on the same network. Get the facilitator. Backup plan: export the sliced file to the printer's SD card or USB and walk it over.
Red warning that the model is outside the plateUse Arrange, or scale the model down. Your design must fit the 60 x 60 x 25 mm challenge box anyway.
Estimate is over 30 minutesScale down 10 to 20 percent, or lower the height. Height costs the most time.
The preview shows supports you didn't ask forTurn supports off, then rotate so the largest flat face sits on the plate. If it still won't print without supports, redesign the overhang.
First layer isn't sticking or looks like spaghettiStop the print from the printer screen. Tell the facilitator. The plate may need cleaning. Your print isn't doomed; it's data.
Parts floating in the air in the previewIn Tinkercad, pieces weren't touching the workplane or each other. Group them, drop them to the workplane, and export again.

Station setup (facilitator)

CheckWhy
Bambu Studio installed on a Windows or Mac laptopIt's a desktop app. It will not run on Chromebooks. Ask district IT early.
Region set correctly on first launch, and logged inAn account from one region can't log in with another region selected.
Bambu Network plug-in installedRequired to log in and to send prints over the network.
Every printer model in the room added as a preset, 0.4 mm nozzle unless you changed itParticipants pick the preset that matches the printer they send to. A mismatched preset can make a print fail or worse.
All printers bound to the logged-in account and on the same network as the stationThat's the only way they appear in the send list.
Label each printer with its model and name as it appears in Bambu Studio"Which one is Printer 2?" is the most-asked question at a slicing station.
Same filament preset as what's loaded (usually generic or Bambu PLA)Wrong filament preset means wrong temperatures.

Classroom note: with students, the pattern that scales is students design and export, the teacher slices. It keeps one person responsible for presets and gives you a natural checkpoint to ask "why this size?"
